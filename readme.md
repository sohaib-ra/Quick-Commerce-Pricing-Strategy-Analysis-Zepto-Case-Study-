# 🛒 Quick Commerce Pricing Strategy: Do Discounts Really Drive Demand?

### A data-driven analysis of whether discounting actually improves revenue in a convenience-driven market

---

## ❗ The Problem

Quick-commerce platforms often rely on discounts to drive growth.

But a critical question remains:

> Are discounts creating real demand — or simply inflating order volume at the cost of revenue efficiency?

---

## 🧠 The Shift

Most pricing strategies assume:

* Lower prices → higher demand → higher revenue

This project tests that assumption in a quick-commerce environment.

---

## 📊 What This Analysis Covers

Using transactional data, this project evaluates:

* How discounts impact order volume
* Whether discounts improve total revenue
* Which products and categories actually drive value
* How price sensitivity varies across categories

---

## 🔍 What the Data Reveals

### 1. 🚚 Demand is Driven by Convenience — Not Price

High-frequency products such as snacks, beverages, and instant food dominate orders.

👉 Customers prioritize speed and availability over discounts

---

### 2. 📦 High Order Volume ≠ High Revenue

Top-selling products are often low-ticket items.

👉 Volume does not necessarily translate into value

---

### 3. 💸 Discounts Do Not Increase Demand Meaningfully

Order volume remains relatively stable across discount levels.

👉 Discounting does not significantly boost demand

---

### 4. 📉 Discounts Reduce Revenue Efficiency

* Revenue is highest at **no discount**
* Revenue per order declines as discounts increase

👉 Discounting reduces transaction-level value

---

### 5. ⚖️ Limited Price Sensitivity Across Categories

Most categories perform optimally at or near full price.

👉 Only a few segments show meaningful response to discounts

---

## 💼 What This Means for the Business

This analysis challenges a key assumption:

> Quick-commerce is not a price-driven marketplace.

Instead:

> It is a **convenience-driven model**, where speed and availability matter more than price.

---

## 🚀 Strategic Implications

* Reduce blanket discounting across categories
* Apply targeted discounts only where price sensitivity exists
* Focus on improving **revenue per order**, not just order volume
* Strengthen positioning around **speed, reliability, and convenience**
